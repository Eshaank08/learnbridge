# Sources
Public videos from universities, maybe translated into different languages by AI. Give clips first to students before they proceed with the full video. This way they can get a quick overview of the topic and decide if they want to dive deeper into it. The clips can be generated by AI based on the content of the videos, highlighting key concepts and important information.

Books, articles, and other written materials can also be included as sources for learning. These can be linked to the nodes in the graph, allowing students to access them easily when they are working on a particular topic.

# Graphs
The base of graph 
Nodes with topics, nodes have hyperlinks to videos, books and other learning materials. Each node can be in one of three states: unlit, lit, or mastered. Unlit nodes are those that the student has not yet attempted to learn. Lit nodes are those that the student has attempted to learn but has not yet mastered. Mastered nodes are those that the student has successfully learned and can demonstrate proficiency in.
To master a node, you would need to take a test on the topic and pass it. The test may be generated by AI based on the content of the videos and books linked to that node.

Connect topics which depend on each other with directed edges. For example, "Calculus" would have a directed edge to "Differential Equations" because understanding calculus is necessary to understand differential equations.

The graph can thus depict a more intertwined structure of knowledge for a roadmad for learning a subject. People would be able to create such self-contained graph-roadmaps for any subject and share them with others. This would allow students to follow a structured learning path and track their progress as they light up and master different nodes in the graph. Additionally, the graph could be updated and expanded over time as new resources become available or as the understanding of the subject evolves.

Next level would be to have a hypergraph for connecting subjects, especially inderdisciplinary ones. For example, "Machine Learning" would have directed edges to both "Statistics" and "Computer Science", indicating that knowledge in both of these areas is important for understanding machine learning. This would allow students to see how different subjects are interconnected and how they can build upon each other to gain a deeper understanding of complex topics.

## Interactivity
Additional feature! Only add after the core is done and funcitons well.
May be add some hidden nodes (or communities of nodes) within graph which are shown only after mastering certain nodes. This would add an element of surprise and discovery to the learning process, encouraging students to explore the graph and uncover new topics as they progress. For example, mastering "Calculus" might unlock a hidden node for "Advanced Calculus" or "Real Analysis", which would provide more in-depth knowledge on the topic. This would create a sense of achievement and motivation for students to continue learning and exploring the graph. At the same time it would allow the graph to look smaller and more doable at the beginning, thus less indimidating for students who are just starting out on their learning journey and as students progress inviting to deepen their knowledge in the subject.
